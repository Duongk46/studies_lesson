3.1
True / False / Not Given
Passage về Urban Green Spaces — 7 câu hỏi
⚠️
Quan trọng — T/F/NG:

TRUE = bài xác nhận. FALSE = bài mâu thuẫn. NOT GIVEN = bài không đề cập (đừng suy luận thêm).

Passage — Urban Green Spaces and Mental Health

Urban green spaces, including parks, gardens, and tree-lined streets, have long been considered desirable features of city environments. However, recent research has begun to quantify their specific effects on mental health outcomes, revealing a more complex picture than previously assumed.

A landmark study conducted by the University of Exeter in 2019 found that people living within 300 metres of green space reported significantly lower levels of stress and anxiety compared to those residing in areas with limited vegetation. The researchers analysed data from over 19,000 participants across England, controlling for socioeconomic factors that might otherwise explain the disparity. Crucially, the benefits persisted regardless of whether participants actively used the green spaces or merely lived in proximity to them.

Not all urban green spaces confer equal benefits, however. Research published in the journal Landscape and Urban Planning suggests that biodiversity plays a critical role in determining the psychological impact of green areas. Participants who visited parks with greater species variety reported higher levels of psychological restoration than those who frequented monoculture green spaces, such as grass-only football pitches. This finding has significant implications for urban planning, suggesting that biodiversity-rich environments should be prioritised in city development.

Critics of the green space hypothesis point to methodological limitations. Many studies rely on self-reported wellbeing data, which is inherently subjective, and longitudinal research tracking individuals over extended periods remains scarce. Moreover, the direction of causality is not always clear — it is possible that mentally healthier individuals are more inclined to seek out and reside near green environments, rather than green spaces directly causing improved mental health.

Despite these caveats, the weight of evidence suggests that urban greenery offers genuine psychological benefits. City planners and policymakers would be well-advised to incorporate green space provisions into urban development strategies, not merely as aesthetic amenities, but as essential infrastructure for public mental health.

Unit 4 — IELTS Writing

IELTS Writing — Bài Mẫu Có Phân Tích Band Score

So sánh bài Band 5.5 và Band 7.5 cùng một đề — phân tích từng câu để hiểu sự khác biệt.

4.1
Task 2 — Band 5.5 vs Band 7.5 (cùng một đề)
Chủ đề: Social Media & Society
Đề thi
"Some people believe that social media has had a predominantly negative impact on individuals and society. To what extent do you agree or disagree? Give reasons for your answer and include any relevant examples from your own knowledge or experience."

Bài Viết — Band 5.5 ❌

Band 5.5 — Weak

Social media is very popular nowadays. Many people use Facebook, Instagram and Twitter every day. I think social media has both good and bad effects on society.

First, social media is bad because it makes people addicted. Many young people spend too much time on their phones and do not study or work. This is a big problem for society.

Second, social media can spread false information. People read news on social media but some of it is not true. This can cause problems in society.

However, social media also has good things. People can communicate with friends and family who live far away. Also, businesses can use social media to advertise their products.

In conclusion, I think social media has more negative effects than positive effects. The government should make rules to control social media use.

Phân Tích Điểm Yếu Band 5.5

Từ vựng nghèo: "very popular", "big problem", "good things", "bad" — tất cả đều quá đơn giản. Không có AWL words.
Thiếu cấu trúc phức tạp: Toàn câu đơn ngắn. Không có relative clauses, participle clauses, hay complex sentences.
Ví dụ không cụ thể: "people read news on social media" — quá chung chung, không có evidence.
Kết nối yếu: "First, Second, However" — discourse markers cơ bản, thiếu đa dạng.
Task Response không rõ: Đề hỏi "to what extent agree/disagree" — bài trả lời mơ hồ, đứng cả hai phía.

Bài Viết — Band 7.5 ✓

Band 7.5 — Strong

Social media platforms have become integral to modern communication, yet their influence on society remains deeply contested. While I acknowledge certain advantages, I firmly believe that the negative ramifications of social media substantially outweigh its benefits, primarily due to its detrimental effects on mental health and the proliferation of misinformation.

The most significant concern is the well-documented link between excessive social media usage and deteriorating mental health, particularly among adolescents. Research conducted by the Royal Society for Public Health found that platforms such as Instagram are associated with increased rates of anxiety, depression, and low self-esteem. The constant exposure to curated, idealised representations of others' lives inevitably fosters social comparison, leading many users to feel chronically inadequate. This psychological toll represents a profound societal cost that cannot be overlooked.

Furthermore, social media has become a breeding ground for misinformation and societal polarisation. Algorithmic content curation prioritises engagement over accuracy, creating echo chambers in which users are repeatedly exposed to sensationalised content. The consequences have been demonstrably severe: during the COVID-19 pandemic, vaccine misinformation spread so rapidly that the WHO declared a parallel "infodemic," directly undermining public health efforts.

Admittedly, social media facilitates global connectivity and has empowered marginalised communities to amplify their voices. Movements such as #MeToo illustrate the platform's capacity for meaningful social change. Nevertheless, these benefits could theoretically be achieved through alternative channels, whereas the psychological and informational harms appear intrinsically tied to the addictive design of these platforms.

In conclusion, while social media offers undeniable connective value, its structural propensity to undermine mental wellbeing and distort public discourse renders it, on balance, a net negative for society. Regulatory intervention and platform redesign are urgently required to mitigate these harms.

Phân Tích Điểm Mạnh Band 7.5

Task Response rõ ràng ngay từ đầu: "I firmly believe that the negative ramifications substantially outweigh its benefits" — lập trường rõ, không mơ hồ.
AWL vocabulary: "ramifications", "proliferation", "detrimental", "demonstrably", "intrinsically", "propensity" — từ học thuật chính xác.
Evidence cụ thể: "Royal Society for Public Health", "WHO declared a parallel infodemic" — evidence có nguồn, không chung chung.
Nhượng bộ (concession) thông minh: Paragraph 4 thừa nhận disadvantage nhưng counter lại — đây là kỹ thuật band 7+.
Cohesion đa dạng: "Furthermore", "Admittedly", "Nevertheless", "In conclusion" — variety tốt, không lặp lại.
Complex grammar: "leading many users to feel", "whereas the psychological harms appear intrinsically tied to" — structures phức tạp, chính xác.

Task 1 — Bar Chart Model Answer với Annotations
Band 7 — Năng lượng tái tạo tại 5 quốc gia
Đề thi (mô phỏng)
The bar chart shows the percentage of electricity generated from renewable sources in five countries (Germany, France, UK, Japan, USA) in 2010 and 2020. Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant.
Band 7 Model Answer

[Introduction — paraphrase đề, không copy]
The bar chart illustrates the proportion of electricity derived from renewable sources across five nations — Germany, France, the United Kingdom, Japan, and the United States — comparing figures from 2010 and 2020.

[Overview — BẮT BUỘC, không có = tối đa Band 5]
Overall, renewable energy generation increased in all five countries over the decade, with Germany recording the most dramatic growth. Japan and the USA, while showing improvement, remained the lowest-ranking nations throughout the period.

[Body 1 — nhóm các nước có điểm nổi bật]
Germany demonstrated the most substantial rise, climbing from approximately 17% in 2010 to around 42% in 2020 — an increase of roughly 25 percentage points. France also saw a notable improvement, growing from 13% to 24%. The United Kingdom experienced moderate growth, rising from 7% to 19% over the same period.

[Body 2 — nhóm còn lại + so sánh]
By contrast, Japan and the USA recorded comparatively modest gains. Japan's share increased marginally from 9% to 15%, while the USA saw a slight rise from 10% to 17%. Despite this growth, both countries remained well below the European nations, with the USA's 2020 figure still lower than Germany's 2010 figure.

Overview technique: Đặt ngay sau introduction, trước body. Nêu 2 main trends — nhóm tăng mạnh và nhóm tăng ít. KHÔNG nêu số liệu cụ thể trong overview.
Data language: "climbing from... to", "roughly 25 percentage points", "comparatively modest gains", "marginally" — variety tốt, không lặp "increased".
Comparison cuối: "the USA's 2020 figure still lower than Germany's 2010 figure" — đây là cross-comparison thông minh, examiners đánh giá cao.
Word count: ~180 words — đúng yêu cầu (150 minimum). Đừng viết quá 200 — quality > quantity.

Band 5:

Band 7.5:

5.2
Part 2 — Cue Card Model Answer (2 phút)
Describe a time you learned something new
Cue Card
Describe a time when you learned something new.
You should say:
— what you learned
— how you learned it
— why you decided to learn it
— and explain how you felt about learning it.
Band 7.5 Model Answer
Opening:
"I'd like to talk about the time I learned to play the guitar, which was about three years ago during the pandemic lockdown."
What + How:
"I'd always admired people who could play instruments, but I'd never had the motivation to actually try. When the lockdown started and I suddenly had a lot of free time on my hands, I thought — why not? I bought a second-hand acoustic guitar and started following online tutorials on YouTube. I practiced for about 45 minutes every evening, starting with basic chords like G, C, and D."
Why:
"My primary motivation was honestly just to stay mentally healthy during what was quite an isolating period. Music has always been a source of comfort for me, and I thought learning to create it myself would be even more therapeutic."
How felt:
"The initial weeks were genuinely frustrating — my fingertips were sore from pressing the strings, and the chord transitions sounded clunky and awkward. But the moment I managed to play my first complete song, I felt an enormous sense of achievement. It's one of those skills where the progress is tangible — you can hear yourself improving, which is incredibly motivating."
Wrap-up:
"Looking back, it's one of the most rewarding things I've done. It taught me that with consistent practice, you can acquire almost any skill — which is a mindset I now apply to many areas of my life."
Time management: 5 blocks, mỗi block ~20-25 giây = tổng ~2 phút chính xác.
Storytelling technique: Specific details ("45 minutes every evening", "G, C, D chords") — làm câu chuyện sống động, không mơ hồ.
Emotion language: "genuinely frustrating", "enormous sense of achievement", "incredibly motivating" — examiner muốn nghe feelings.
Reflection cuối: "Looking back, it taught me that..." — câu kết lấy điểm cao vì show depth of thought.

Part 3 — Abstract Discussion Model Answers
Chủ đề: Learning & Education
Q:
"Do you think formal education is more important than self-directed learning?"
A (7.5):
"That's a fascinating tension, actually. I'd argue that the two are not mutually exclusive — in fact, the most effective learners tend to combine both. Formal education provides a structured foundation and credentials that open professional doors, which is undeniably important in a competitive job market. However, in a world where knowledge evolves so rapidly, formal curricula often lag behind industry needs. Self-directed learning fills that gap. Personally, I think the ability to teach yourself new skills is arguably more valuable than any degree in the long run."
✓ Direct answer → nuanced position → both sides → personal view → strong conclusion.
Q:
"How has the internet changed the way people learn compared to previous generations?"
A (7.5):
"The shift has been nothing short of revolutionary, I'd say. Previous generations were largely constrained by access — if you didn't live near a good library or university, your learning options were severely limited. The internet has democratised knowledge in an unprecedented way. Any motivated individual can now access world-class lectures from MIT or Harvard for free. That said, information abundance brings its own challenge: the ability to evaluate sources critically has become more important than ever. So while the internet has removed barriers to knowledge, it's placed greater demand on learners to develop their own judgment."
✓ Uses contrast (previous vs now). AWL words: "constrained", "unprecedented", "democratised". Concession structure. Strong ending.

Unit 7 — Hội Thoại Thực Tế

Professional Dialogues — Framework trong Thực Tế

Xem các framework (PREP, ACS, DESC) được dùng trong hội thoại thực tế — chú ý cách chuyển tiếp tự nhiên.

7.1
Disagreeing in a Meeting — ACS Framework
Business meeting: budget discussion
Manager:
"I think we should cut the training budget by 40% this quarter. We need to focus resources on the product launch."
You (ACS):
[A — Acknowledge] "I completely understand the priority around the launch — it's clearly critical for Q3 targets. [C — Counter] That said, I'm a bit concerned about the timing of a training cut. Our team has been dealing with a fairly steep learning curve on the new CRM, and reducing support now could actually slow down the launch preparation itself. [S — State view] My suggestion would be to keep the training budget intact this quarter and look at optimising it in Q4 once the launch pressure eases."
ACS = Acknowledge → Counter → State. Không reject thẳng, không aggressive, nhưng vẫn nêu quan điểm rõ ràng.
Manager:
"That's a fair point about the CRM. What if we defer the cut rather than cancel it?"
You:
"That works for me. A deferral to Q4 gives us the runway we need. I can put together a quick analysis of training ROI to support that recommendation for the finance committee if that would help."
✓ Accept compromise gracefully. Offer to help = builds credibility. Volunteering = proactive professional.
7.2
Giving Difficult Feedback — DESC Script
1-on-1 với đồng nghiệp về vấn đề deadline
You (DESC):
[D — Describe] "Hey James, do you have a few minutes? I wanted to talk about the last three project reports. [E — Express] When they came in after the Friday 5pm deadline, I found it quite difficult to review them properly before our Monday client call — which put me in a bit of a tough spot. [S — Specify] What I'd really appreciate going forward is having them by 3pm on Fridays instead. [C — Consequence] That way I can give you more thorough feedback, and our presentations will be much stronger for the client."
DESC = Describe (behavior, not person) → Express (feelings) → Specify (request) → Consequence (benefit). Không blame, không emotional.
James:
"You're right, I'm sorry about that. Fridays are hectic for me. Would it work if I sent a draft by 3pm and flagged any parts that need more time?"
You:
"That sounds like a great solution, actually. A draft with flags is much easier to work with than nothing at all. Let's go with that."
✓ Accept compromise. "That sounds like a great solution" = genuine acknowledgement, not patronising.
7.3
Networking Small Talk — FORD Method
Conference event, gặp người lần đầu
You:
"Hi, I don't think we've met — I'm Minh, I work in product development at TechBase."
Sarah:
"Oh nice to meet you, Minh. I'm Sarah — I'm in UX design over at Meridian."
You (O):
"UX design — that's fascinating. How long have you been in that space?"
O = Occupation. Đặt câu hỏi mở, không yes/no.
Sarah:
"About six years now. I started in graphic design and sort of evolved into UX. Are you enjoying the conference?"
You:
"I really am — the morning session on AI integration was eye-opening. What did you think of it? Given your background in UX, you'd have a pretty interesting take on the human-centred design angle."
✓ Follow-up question tailored to THEIR expertise → họ cảm thấy được trân trọng, sẽ mở ra nhiều hơn.
Sarah:
"Ha, yes — I had mixed feelings. The AI capabilities are impressive, but I worry designers are being sold a black box they don't fully understand..."
You (kết thúc):
"That's a really valid concern — I'd love to hear more about your perspective. Would you be up for grabbing coffee during the break? I think there's a lot our teams could learn from each other."
✓ Kết thúc với concrete next step (coffee). Không chỉ "let's keep in touch" — quá vague.
